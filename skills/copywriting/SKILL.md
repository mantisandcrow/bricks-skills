# Copywriting Playbook — Mantis & Crow

> The agency's reusable methodology for writing copy that earns attention, holds it, and converts — across our own site, client sites, proposals, and case studies.

## When this skill applies

Load when the task is any of:

- Drafting or rewriting headlines, taglines, sub-heads
- Writing or revising body copy (homepage, landing pages, about, services, contact)
- Authoring case study narratives or portfolio blurbs
- Writing blog posts, articles, or thought-leadership pieces
- Building CTA banks and button labels
- Writing bios (founder, team, client)
- Sales-page or proposal copy
- Social posts or captions where voice matters
- Editing existing copy for tightness, tone, or conversion
- Naming a project, product, or campaign
- Reviewing or critiquing copy through the three lenses

If the request is "make this sound better" or "run this through the lenses" or mentions any lens name, this skill applies.

## Brand voice — Mantis & Crow

The agency's house voice. Proven working lines anchor the voice — every new piece should sound like it could sit next to these on the site.

### Anchor lines (the house, in their own words)

- **"Your team. Without the hire."** — the master headline. Names the pain (recruiting, onboarding, payroll, management) and promises the outcome. Beats the alternatives ("Zero overhead" = CFO-speak; "Your digital team" = too vague).
- **"Not a vendor. A team."** — the relationship frame.
- **"Built. Shipped. Selling."** — replaces the generic "Built. Launched. Grown." Specificity wins (selling > grown).
- **"Every company deserves a web team. Not a vendor. Not a project. A team. Yours."** — Steve-Jobs-style keynote close.
- **"Your Website Should Make Money. Not Just Exist."** — the Steve-Jobs one-liner.
- **"Your Website Is Either An Asset Or A Headache."** — the binary frame.
- **"Outcomes, Not Output."** — the B2B closer (replaces "Trusted By Teams Who Measure In Revenue, Not Hours").
- **"We replace your web team... without the firing."** — the conversation-starter.

### Principles

- **Sharp, not slick.** Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Cut adverbs.
- **Earned confidence.** State outcomes without hedging. Don't apologise for asking for the sale.
- **Specific over generic.** Replace "solutions" with what they actually do. Numbers beat adjectives.
- **Plain English.** Avoid jargon unless the audience uses it. "WebOps" and "AEO" are acceptable inside the agency; outside, prefer "embedded web team" and "AI search visibility."
- **Short sentences. Mix with one longer one for rhythm.**
- **Second person ("you")** for reader-facing copy; **first person ("we")** for the agency's own story; **third person** for clients and case studies.
- **Three-beat rhythm works.** `Built. Shipped. Selling.` `Not a vendor. A team.` `Your team. Without the hire.` Use it when the structure can carry it. Don't force it.

### House style

- Sentence case for headings (not Title Case unless quoted)
- Oxford comma in lists of three or more
- Numbers under 10 spelled out; 10+ as numerals
- `%` with no space ("12%", not "12 %")
- Currency: `R12,000` (ZAR) / `USD 12,000` / `€12,000` — match client market
- One space after periods (never two)
- Curly quotes only if the rest of the site uses them; otherwise straight
- Dates: `29 September 2026` (no comma) for narrative; `2026-09-29` for ISO contexts

### Words we avoid

- "solutions", "leverage", "synergy", "ecosystem" (of services), "best-in-class", "world-class", "cutting-edge"
- "We help businesses..." — always say what you do, not the abstraction
- "Passionate", "dedicated", "committed" — show it, don't claim it
- "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "disruptive" — never
- "Just" before a verb (just ship, just build) — delete "just"
- "Zero overhead" in headlines — sells savings, not capability. Use "Without the hire" instead.
- Em-dash decoration (—) — overuse reads as LinkedIn cadence. Periods, commas, colons, or restructure. Use em-dashes only when the rhythm genuinely needs one and only once per section.

### Words we like

- "Built", "shipped", "launched", "drove", "moved", "owned"
- Concrete outcomes: "12% lift", "shipped in 6 weeks", "replaced their CRM"
- "Honest", "useful", "specific", "fast", "yours"

## The Three-Lens Framework (the agency's house method)

Every piece of Mantis & Crow copy gets reviewed (or drafted) through three lenses before shipping. The lenses are not a checklist — they're three distinct vantage points, and the strongest copy usually combines all three.

The agency default leans **Steve (vision)** with **Chris (specificity)** as the floor and **Gary (urgency)** reserved for high-stakes moments (CTAs, deadline moments, scarcity).

### Lens 1 — Chris Orzechowski (conversion & specificity)

**Core principle:** Specificity converts. Name the pain, the timeline, the mechanism. Specifics beat cleverness. One CTA per section. One idea per paragraph.

**When to use:** Headlines, sub-heads, body copy, CTAs, FAQ answers, case study intros — anything that needs to drive action or hold attention.

**Voice rules:**
- Lead with the outcome or a sharp claim
- Use concrete numbers, named tools, named timelines
- Replace vague verbs with specific ones ("improve" → "lift trial sign-ups 12%")
- One idea per paragraph; one CTA per view
- Open with the strongest sentence (don't bury the lede)
- "Buried the lede" is the most common Chris-lens failure

**Chris-lens prompts to ask of any draft:**
- Does the headline stand alone, out of context?
- Is the pain named, or just implied?
- Is the timeline specific, or "fast" / "quick"?
- Is the CTA a verb-led action or "Learn more"?

### Lens 2 — Gary Halbert (greed, fear, urgency, memorability)

**Core principle:** People move on greed and fear. Be memorable, be aggressive, name the competitor. If the reader can forget the line in 5 minutes, it's not finished.

**When to use:** Final CTAs, deadline copy, scarcity plays, hero banners where the prospect is comparison-shopping, business cards, taglines, push notifications, sales emails, "before your competitor does" frames.

**Voice rules:**
- Activate fear (loss) and greed (gain) — they're the only buttons that move humans
- Name the competitor explicitly — "Your competitors started yesterday"
- Specific numbers beat adjectives ("$400,000 team" beats "expensive team")
- Aggressive is fine. Cruel is not.
- Punchy > clever. "Get the free audit before your competitor does" beats "Reach out today"
- The line must be quotable — could a competitor's marketing person retweet it?

**Gary-lens prompts to ask of any draft:**
- Would this line make the prospect feel urgency, or just information?
- Is the cost of inaction named, or only the benefit of action?
- Is there a specific number, name, or timeline that creates pressure?
- Could a prospect quote this to a friend? (If no, it's forgettable.)

### Lens 3 — Steve Jobs (vision, relationship, philosophy)

**Core principle:** Sell the relationship, not the feature. Name what the prospect deserves. Vision beats description. Three beats beat three paragraphs.

**When to use:** Brand voice, "about" sections, philosophy paragraphs, keynote-style opens, "one more thing" reveals, relationship frames in sales conversations, the closing line above the final CTA.

**Voice rules:**
- Talk about *you* (the prospect), not *us* (the agency)
- "There is something wrong with the way..." is the setup
- "We think there's a better way." is the reveal
- Three beats: `Not a vendor. A team.` / `Built. Shipped. Selling.` / `Built. Live. Working.`
- Avoid adjectives. Avoid adverbs. Use nouns and verbs.
- "What if your web team showed up before you needed them?" — questions that reframe the prospect's world
- The strongest Steve-Jobs copy says the thing no one else is saying

**Steve-lens prompts to ask of any draft:**
- Is this about the prospect, or about us?
- Is there a noun and a verb doing the work, or adjectives and adverbs?
- Could the line be shorter without losing meaning?
- Does it reframe how the prospect sees their own problem?

### How to apply the three lenses

**Option A — Draft then review.** Write the piece. Run it through each lens in order. Rewrite the weak spots. Most agency copy lives here.

**Option B — Lens-led drafting.** Pick the lens that fits the section's job. Draft in that voice. Then check the other two for what's missing. Best for hero copy, CTAs, and tagline work.

**Option C — Three drafts, pick the strongest.** Write three versions of the same line, one per lens. The "winner" is usually a hybrid. Use this for high-stakes lines (the hero H1, the closing CTA, the agency tagline).

**Default combo:** Steve opens the relationship, Chris delivers the mechanism, Gary closes the urgency. Most landing pages follow this rhythm even when the sections aren't labelled.

## Core principles (apply to all copy, regardless of lens)

1. **Lead with the outcome, not the process.** "We shipped a CRM that pays for itself in 4 months" beats "We built a custom CRM using Laravel."
2. **One idea per paragraph. One claim per section.**
3. **Cut 20% in revision.** Every first draft is too long. Be ruthless.
4. **Show, don't claim.** Replace "experienced team" with a specific number ("18 launches across retail, fintech, and healthcare").
5. **The reader is busy.** Front-load the value. The fold is the test.
6. **One CTA per page section.** Two CTAs in the same view splits attention.
7. **No filler transitions.** "Furthermore", "Moreover", "In addition" — delete on sight.
8. **Headings are scannable, not clever.** A heading that needs explanation is a sentence in the wrong place.
9. **The last sentence of every section is the bridge to the next.** If it's a dead end, fix the order.
10. **Read it aloud.** If you wouldn't say it, don't write it.

## Copy types — structures that work

### Hero headlines (H1)

The agency default is two lines. The first line does the Steve work (relationship/outcome); the second line does the Chris work (specificity/mechanism).

- **Formulas that hold up:**
  - `[Verb] [Who] [Outcome].` — "Built. Shipped. Selling."
  - `[Outcome] without [objection].` — "Your team. Without the hire."
  - `How [Who] [Outcome].` — for case studies and articles
  - `[Number] [Things] that [Outcome].` — "6 case studies that drove real results."
  - The Steve keynote opener: `[Existing problem]. We think there's a better way.`
  - The Gary urgency opener: `Your competitors [action] yesterday.`
- **Never** open a headline with a question. A question is a craft choice, not a copy choice.
- **Never** use "Zero overhead" in headlines. Use "Without the hire."

### Sub-headlines (H2, supporting)

- One sentence that *adds context the H1 didn't*
- Specifics over generalities: name the audience, name the outcome

### Body copy (paragraphs, sections)

- 2–4 sentences per paragraph
- Open with the strongest sentence (don't bury the lede)
- One claim, one example, one implication
- Bold the load-bearing words (1–3 per paragraph max)
- Break up long sections with an image, a pull-quote, or a sub-head every 80–120 words

### CTAs (buttons, links)

- Lead with the verb: "Get the proposal", "Book a 20-minute call", "See the case study"
- **Never** "Click here", "Submit", "Learn more" alone
- Match the offer to the friction:
  - low-friction → "Read more"
  - medium-friction → "See the proposal"
  - high-friction → "Book a call"
  - conversion → "Claim your free audit →"
- Always second person ("Get" not "Get yours")
- For the final CTA, add a Gary-lens urgency line ("Claim your free audit before your competitor does →")

### About / bios

- Open with the *what* — "Gareth runs Mantis & Crow, a small agency that ships websites in 30 days." — not the *who*
- Three sections: present → past → proof
- End on proof (a number, a name, a launch)

### Case study narratives — the Flow Ninja template

The agency uses the Flow Ninja case study structure: metric-or-transformation H1, stats row, client quote, intro, challenge, approach, results, project details, next-study CTA.

When post-launch metrics don't exist yet, pivot the angle from "results we drove" to "transformation we delivered" — process and scope metrics stand in for performance metrics. Update the stats when data arrives.

**Full structure** (see "Case study template" below for the full layout and copy patterns).

### Sales pages (proposals, landing pages)

- Same structure as a long case study, aimed at *the buyer's fear*, not the agency's process
- Three sections that always earn their place: outcome → proof → risk reversal
- Risk reversal: timeline, payment terms, what's included, what happens next

### Blog posts / articles

- Hook in the first sentence (a number, a name, a claim)
- One idea per H2 section
- End on a *useful* sentence, not "In conclusion"
- Internal links to relevant case studies, services, or posts — at least 2

### Social posts / captions

- First line carries the post (it shows above the fold)
- Hashtags: 1–3 on LinkedIn, 5–10 on Instagram, 0–2 on Facebook
- One ask per post

### Email subject lines

- `[Client] — proposal for [scope]`
- `[Outcome] in [timeframe]? Here's how.`
- Never: `Quick question`, `Following up`, `Just checking in`

## Process — the agency's writing loop

1. **Brief** — write 3 bullets: outcome, audience, voice (3 adjectives, e.g. "sharp, specific, unsentimental"). One sentence each.
2. **Pick the lens** — which lens dominates this section? (Default: Steve to open, Chris to deliver, Gary to close.)
3. **Draft** — write the bad version first. Get it on the page.
4. **Run the three lenses** — review through Chris (specific), Gary (urgent), Steve (vision). Note the weakest line in each.
5. **Cut** — read aloud. Cut every word that doesn't carry weight. Cut 20%.
6. **Test** — check the headline out of context. Does it stand alone? If not, rewrite.
7. **Refine** — read for rhythm, then for grammar, then for one last pass for filler.
8. **Hand off** — file any new decisions (a tagline, a house-style exception, a lesson) via `mempalace_checkpoint`.

## SEO-aware copy (handoff from seo-playbook)

- Lead paragraph of every page must contain the **primary keyword** and the **audience descriptor**.
- Title tag and H1 should match intent but not be identical (Google prefers H1 to differ slightly from `<title>`).
- Meta description: 140–158 chars, includes the primary keyword, ends with a verb-led CTA.
- Internal links: at least 2 contextual links from any post going >300 words.
- Sub-headings (H2/H3) carry secondary keywords naturally — don't force them.

## AEO-aware copy (handoff from aeo-playbook)

- The first sentence of a section is the *answer*. LLMs quote it.
- Use definitions: "X is the practice of..." — gives the LLM a quotable sentence.
- Schema-friendly structure: FAQ blocks, HowTo steps, comparison tables.
- Author bylines + publication dates visible on every post.
- Avoid rhetorical questions in the first paragraph — LLMs misquote them as fact.
- **FAQ pattern (proven):** Heading `Ask Us Anything`, subhead `How we work. What you pay. Why [service] matters. No jargon, no pitch.` Each Q&A: first sentence = the answer (AEO snippet gold). Format: `Q: [question]\nA: [one-sentence answer, then the rest].`

## Anti-patterns — kill on sight

- **AI tells:** "In today's fast-paced world", "It's no secret that", "Let's dive in", "Game-changer", "Unlock the power of"
- **Hedge stacks:** "may potentially help to possibly..." — pick a verb
- **Buzzword stacks:** "innovative, scalable, robust solutions" — name what it actually does
- **Vague claims:** "We deliver results" — *what* results?
- **Stacked adjectives:** "powerful, intuitive, and elegant platform" — pick one, prove it
- **Passive voice where active works:** "was built by our team" → "our team built it"
- **Filler transitions:** "Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally"
- **Openers that don't open:** "So", "Well", "Okay" — delete
- **"Really":** delete it
- **"Zero overhead" in headlines** — sells savings, not capability. Use "Without the hire."
- **"Your digital team"** — too vague. Use "Your team."
- **Em-dash decoration (—):** overuse is a LinkedIn cadence tell. Use periods, commas, or restructure. One per section max.
- **Generic action verbs in CTAs:** "Learn more", "Submit", "Find out" — replace with verb-led specifics.

## Templates

### Hero headline (two-line)

```
Line 1 — sharper, concrete version of the claim (often outlined or italic; Steve-lens relationship)
Line 2 — the punchline (often solid or condensed; Chris-lens mechanism)
```

Example: `Built. Shipped. Selling.` / `Your team. Without the hire.`

### Service description (3-sentence block)

```
We [do what] for [audience], [in what timeframe/way].
Each [deliverable] is [concrete differentiator].
[Proof: number, name, or launch].
```

### Bio (founder / team)

```
[Name] [role] [agency/company]. [One-line credential — name, number, or launch].
Before that, [one-line career anchor].
[Optional: one humanising line].
```

### Case study one-liner (for portfolio cards)

```
[Client] — [outcome] [proof].
```

Example: `Burnish — 3x trial sign-ups in 90 days.`

### CTA banks

| Friction | CTA |
|---|---|
| Low (read more) | Read the case study |
| Medium (get a sample) | See the proposal |
| High (book a call) | Book a 20-minute call |
| Conversion (quote) | Get a fixed-price quote |
| Final CTA (urgency) | Claim your free audit → |
| Final CTA (urgency, alt) | Claim your free audit before your competitor does → |
| Final CTA (vision) | Let's see if we're a fit → |

### Headline formula variants

| Variant | Lens | Use when | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verb + audience + outcome | Chris | Service pages, hero | `Built. Shipped. Selling.` |
| Outcome without objection | Chris | Address a known fear | `Your team. Without the hire.` |
| How + audience + outcome | Chris | Case study hero | `How Burnish tripled trial sign-ups.` |
| Number + noun + outcome | Chris | Listicle, proof page | `6 case studies that drove real results.` |
| Three beats (verb. verb. verb.) | Steve | Tagline, brand voice | `Not a vendor. A team.` |
| Binary frame | Steve | Re-frame the prospect's world | `Your website is either an asset or a headache.` |
| Competitor urgency | Gary | Final CTA, deadline copy | `Your competitors started yesterday.` |
| One-more-thing reveal | Steve | Keynote-style opens | `What if your team showed up before you needed them?` |

### FAQ AEO pattern (proven for SEO + AI citation)

```
Heading: Ask Us Anything
Subhead: How we work. What you pay. Why [topic] matters. No jargon, no pitch.

Q: [Real question a prospect asks]
A: [One-sentence answer — this is the AEO snippet, written to be quoted out of context.] [Then the explanation, 1–2 sentences.]
```

AEO-optimised answers lead with the definition or the answer, not the framing.

## Case study template — the Flow Ninja structure

Used across all Mantis & Crow case studies. Each section's copy patterns below.

### Section 1 — Hero

- **H1:** `[Metric result] OR [transformation delivered] for [client].`
  - With data: `Achieving 21.4% YoY demo request increase across 5 markets.`
  - Without data (pre-launch): `From Generic Provider To Trusted Partner — A Complete Digital Transformation For TestWise.`
- **Stats row:** 3–4 KPIs. If no performance data yet, use process + scope metrics: `WebOps · Brand Positioning · Content Strategy · Web Development`.
- **Client quote:** 1 sentence, anchored to the *transformation*, not the relationship. "They understood that our work isn't about testing. It's about trust." works better than "Great team to work with."

### Section 2 — Intro

1 paragraph (3–5 sentences): who the client is, what they did before, what we did, the result. End with the project tag list (services rendered).

### Section 3 — The Challenge

- **Subhead:** A specific, pain-anchored title. `"A Credible Service With A Forgettable Presence."` beats `"The Challenge."`
- 1 short paragraph framing the *business* problem (not the *website* problem).
- 3–5 bullet points of the specific issues. Use the client's own vocabulary where possible.

### Section 4 — The Approach

- **Subhead:** Frames the work as a *reframe*, not a task. `"We Didn't Redesign A Website. We Redesigned The Conversation."`
- 3–5 sub-sections, each: `What we did` (specific) + `Why it mattered` (business outcome). Best formatted as a 2-column table.

### Section 5 — The Result

- **Subhead:** Mirror the H1 verb. `"A Site That Earns Trust Before The First Call."`
- 1 short paragraph. Specific outcomes, even if qualitative. Use `[Update this section with actual metrics when available: organic traffic, conversion rate, booking volume, time on site, etc.]` as a placeholder when data is pending.

### Section 6 — Project Details

Table: `Client | Industry | Services | Timeline | Platform | Team`.

### Section 7 — Next Case Study / CTA

- Mirror the hero structure of the next case study, OR
- `Your Site Should Work As Hard As Your Business.` / `We build digital experiences that turn visitors into believers. Let's talk about yours.` / `[Start Your Project →]`

### Case study update rule

When post-launch metrics arrive, update Section 5 (The Result) first, then Section 1 (Hero H1 + stats row) to lead with the new data. Tag the case study as `[Updated YYYY-MM-DD]` in the project file so the team knows when it was last refreshed.

## MemPalace filing — when to file what

When the writing session produces a durable decision:

- **New house-style exception** (e.g. a client uses Title Case in headings) → `agency-services/marketing` room
- **A tagline or copy decision for a specific client** → that client's wing, `content` room
- **A real lesson from a session** (e.g. "em-dash overuse reads as LinkedIn cadence") → `agency-services/lessons`
- **A reusable framework** discovered during client work (e.g. a new lens combination that worked) → `agency-services/marketing`
- **A validated copy line** (a tagline that survived A/B testing or client review) → `agency-services/marketing`

Use `mempalace_checkpoint` with a single batch call — don't dribble drawers one at a time.

## Updating this skill

This skill is the agency's house methodology. Update it when:

- A new copywriting framework is adopted across more than one project
- A house-style rule changes (e.g. em-dash policy, "zero overhead" rule)
- A new lens combination proves out across multiple projects
- A common client request reveals a gap (e.g. "we need a 60-second elevator pitch template")

Do **not** edit this skill for one client's voice — that's a client-wing drawer, not agency methodology.
